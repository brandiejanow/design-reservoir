Knowledge
of Design

What does design allow us to know, and how does that knowledge develop, travel and remain available?

Design can be studied as a body of knowledge created through questions, evidence, making, use, documentation and time. This inquiry connects different disciplinary methods without reducing design to one profession or one kind of output.

01 / The position

Knowledge exists beyond the finished object.

A building, product, digital service, image, prototype, dataset, collection or public space may each contain the results of design decisions. Yet the questions, investigations, experiments and judgments that produced it are also valuable forms of knowledge.

Design is understood here not only as a profession or a set of disciplines, but as a way of investigating problems, connecting evidence, making proposals and learning from what enters the world.

This proposition provides a direction for research. Different areas of practice will require different methods and standards of evidence.

02 / How inquiry moves

A connected framework for learning

The 2026 Knowledge of Design intellectual framework follows an inquiry through seven related stages. These are relationships, not a rigid sequence that every project must follow.

  1. CuriosityWhat are we trying to understand? A question may begin in an observation, material, behavior, pattern or tension.
  2. Ways of knowingWhich scientific, technical, engineering, artistic or mathematical approaches can help?
  3. Design and creativityHow do different forms of knowledge inform the same inquiry?
  4. Research and makingWhat can be learned through evidence, experiments, prototyping and documentation?
  5. Futures and timeWhich possibilities, risks and consequences become visible over time?
  6. Life and responsibilityHow do decisions affect people, other living systems, resources and future conditions?
  7. ContinuityHow can what was learned remain accessible, interpretable and open to future inquiry?

03 / STEAM as ways of knowing

Different fields answer different questions.

Science, technology, engineering, arts and mathematics contribute different tools, forms of evidence, languages and ways of understanding. Design often brings several of them into a shared problem without erasing their separate expertise.

S

Science

Observation, experiment, testable explanation and the study of material and living systems.

T

Technology

Tools, computation, digital infrastructure and methods for producing and extending capability.

E

Engineering

Technical systems, constraints, performance, construction, reliability and optimization.

A

Arts

Interpretation, expression, perception, critique, narrative and cultural inquiry.

M

Mathematics

Models, measurement, geometry, patterns and formal relationships.

+

Hybrid disciplines

Architecture, conservation, design research, anthropology, public policy and others move among these ways of knowing.

These are working descriptions for a cross-disciplinary framework, not claims that a field belongs exclusively in any one column.

04 / Knowledge through practice

What research and making reveal

Design inquiry draws on observation, interviews, qualitative coding, archival study, prototyping, measurement, material trials, simulation, critique, use and revision. A method becomes meaningful through the question it can answer and the evidence it preserves.

Making as investigation

A material, prototype or working system can reveal behavior and constraints that a description cannot.

Dialogue as research

Recorded and reviewed conversations can produce evidence when methods, interpretation and limits are documented.

Public encounter

How people use, maintain, adapt or question a design creates further knowledge after its introduction.

Documentation and critique

Preserved reasoning allows later researchers and practitioners to understand, challenge and extend the work.

05 / Futures and living systems

Knowledge must extend beyond the present.

Futures methods investigate several plausible directions, with attention to weak signals, established patterns, uncertainty and choices. A scenario is a structured possibility, not a forecast or an observed fact.

Life-centric thinking broadens the question of design's consequences. Ecological, material, human and more-than-human conditions must be considered in the same inquiry as performance, economy and cultural meaning.

06 / GLAM and continuity

Knowledge needs a life beyond its creation.

Galleries, libraries, archives and museums collect, conserve, catalog, study, interpret and circulate records across generations. Their holdings are not limited to art: they also preserve evidence of scientific processes, technological change, craft, construction, engineering, mathematics and social life.

Within this research, GLAM is treated as a cross-cutting knowledge system. It makes earlier investigations available to new ones while raising questions about classification, access, provenance, digitization and whose contributions enter the record.

07 / Continuing research

Questions we are still developing

The Knowledge of Design research agenda organizes eight ongoing streams. They provide a structure for future studies without presenting open questions as settled findings.

Design as knowledge

How is learning generated and shared through research, making and public use?

STEAM as a knowledge system

Where do ways of knowing meet and where must disciplinary differences remain?

GLAM and continuity

How do institutions preserve and circulate knowledge across fields?

Design culture

What allows original practice, technical confidence and shared standards to develop?

Futures and signals

Which changes require further evidence, and what possibilities do they suggest?

Life-centric design

How are consequences for living systems brought into design choices?

Institutions and public life

What creates lasting access to knowledge and opportunities to use it?

Cultural intelligence

How can quantitative traces and cultural interpretation be studied together?

The next stages are further reading, cross-disciplinary source mapping, comparative research and publication of evidence with clear methods and limits.
